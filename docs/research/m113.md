# M113: March tax-loss selling on M106 (pre-registered 2026-10-04)

Asked by the user (M110 follow-up idea 2). India has no wash-sale rule: a holding sold at a loss can be bought back at
once and the loss still counts. Selling losers just before the financial year ends turns paper losses into realised
losses that cut that year's tax (or carry forward), at the cost of one round trip. Variant B also uses the yearly
equity long-term exemption (Rs 1.25 lakh): long-term equity gains up to the unused exemption are realised tax-free and
the cost basis steps up.

## Mechanics (`src/mb/evaluation/tax.py: harvest`, settings in `configs/tax/harvest_*.yaml`)

The strategy's trades are unchanged. Each March, on the second-last NSE session of the month (T):
- every symbol whose open lots, valued at T's close net of sell costs, are below their cost basis by at least
  `loss_min_frac` (2%) is sold at T's close;
- (B only) then equity lots held over 365 days with a gain are sold, oldest first, while the year's realised equity
  long-term gains stay within the exemption;
- the same shares are bought back at the next session's open (T+1, still in March). Buying back the same day is
  avoided: a same-day sell and buy nets out in settlement and can be treated as intraday (speculative) trading.
Cost: slippage, charges and the DP fee from the market config on both legs, plus the overnight move from T's close
to T+1's open. The book is scaled down by that cost from T+1 on (the same linear scaling as the M110 tax model).
The re-bought lots start a new holding period (FIFO).
