# M119: a large-cap options book, F&O stocks ranked by option signals (pre-registered 2026-10-14)

Asked by the user ("yes build the large-cap options book"), after M118 showed per-stock option signals cannot touch
M106: its picks are almost never F&O stocks. This is a separate book on the stocks that have listed options, meant to
sit beside M106 as a second sleeve.

## Data and checks (no returns used)

`data/raw/nse_fo_bhav` (NSE F&O bhavcopy 2009-2026, from M118). Per contract and day: close, volume (contracts), open
interest, and `val_lakh`, which is (strike + premium) x shares traded in both the old and the UDiFF files (checked on
INFY 2015 and 2025; the lot size itself is missing from the old files).

Implied volatility: Black-76 on each option's close, with the close of the same expiry's stock future as the forward,
so no dividend or borrow guess is needed; discount rate 6.5% a year (it only scales both legs by e^{-rT}). Only
contracts that traded that day (vol > 0) are used: untraded rows carry an old or theoretical price. NSE stock options
were American-style until about 2011; near the money the early-exercise premium is small and is ignored.

Checks run before writing this (code in `src/mb/data/fno.py`):
- Against Dhan's 1-minute ATM option series (118 stocks, month-ends Nov 2021 to Sep 2026, 15:29 bar): option closes
  agree within 1.5% (median); the average of call and put IV at the same near-ATM strike correlates 0.994 with
  Dhan's, median gap 0.3 vol points (2,169 stock-days). Dhan's call-put IV gap is not comparable: it is set by Dhan's
  spot and rate model (median -2.9 points), while with the future as forward ours is centred near zero (median +0.3,
  median size 0.4 points). The spread signal is therefore "options against the stock future", not "options against
  spot".
- Coverage at month-ends (stocks with a traded call and put at one strike within 0.9-1.1 of the forward): about 80
  (2010), 110 (2012-13), 125 (2014), 190 (2022-26). Enough for a top fifth of 15-40 names.
- F&O symbols are the symbol of the day; the panel uses the latest symbol. Old symbols are mapped through the
  dataset's `rename_links` (e.g. TATAMOTORS -> TMPV, MCDOWELL-N -> UNITDSPR).

A value counts from the day after its F&O session (NSE publishes in the evening), as in M118: a decision on d uses the
latest F&O session before d.

## The book

Monthly, at the session `decision_offset_sessions` before each month's last session (as M106). Traded at the next
open, nse_cash costs, fully invested, no regime filter, no gold.

Universe at d: stocks with all three signals below on the F&O session before d, and an EQ bar in the panel on d.
Every candidate and the baseline use this same universe, so the signal is the only difference.

Signals (all from that one F&O session t; expiry = the nearest with a traded future at least 10 calendar days after t):
1. `call_put_spread` (Cremers & Weinbaum 2010): over the strikes within 0.9-1.1 of the forward where both the call and
   the put traded, the open-interest-weighted mean of call IV minus put IV (weight: the pair's mean OI). High buys.
2. `skew` (Xing, Zhang & Zhao 2010): IV of the put with strike/forward closest to 0.95 in [0.80, 0.95), minus IV of the
   call closest to 1.0 in [0.95, 1.05]. Low buys.
3. `option_volume` (Johnson & So 2012): shares traded in all the stock's options over the 21 F&O sessions to t,
   divided by shares traded in the stock (panel EQ volume) over the 21 panel sessions to t. Low buys.
4. `combo`: the mean of the three percentile ranks, each oriented so high buys. High buys.

Picks: the best ceil(20%) of the universe by the signal, equal weight. Baseline `ew`: the whole universe, equal
weight. All parameters in the configs; none tuned. Signal directions are the papers', fixed here.

Information, before the backtests (no returns): universe size by year; the cross-sectional Spearman correlation of the
signals, averaged over decisions; monthly one-way turnover of each candidate's picks.

## Pass (dev 2010-01-04..2023-12-31, offsets 0/5/10/15)

Stage 1, does the signal pick better stocks than holding them all (each candidate vs `ew` at the same offset):
- whole-period Calmar before tax higher at all four offsets, after tax (M110 model) higher at offset 0, and before tax
  higher on at least 2 of f1/f2/f3 at offset 0.

Stage 2, does it help next to M106 (only candidates passing stage 1): a blend, 75% M106 + 25% the book at the start,
not rebalanced between them (as `mb.strategy.blend`), tax on the two books' gains together:
- offset 0 (M106: run 20261002-050950-93574646): blend Calmar before and after tax higher than M106 alone, before tax
  on at least 2 of 3 folds;
- offsets 5/10/15 (M106: `m111_ref_offN`): blend before-tax Calmar higher than M106 at all three.

Locked look, 2024-01-01..2026-09-28 at offset 0, for the one candidate with the higher mean before-tax blend Calmar
over the four offsets (M106 final run 20261002-054052-94c22192): blend Calmar before and after tax not lower than M106.

For information, not a candidate: the stage 2 blend with `ew`, the plain large-cap book.

Expectation: weak. The papers measure weekly or monthly long-short returns in US stocks; this is long-only, Indian
large caps, end-of-day closes that are not synchronous (the future's close is a last-half-hour average, an option's
the last trade). A large-cap long-only book will likely have a lower Calmar than M106 and fall with it in crashes, so
the blend test is hard to pass. Four candidates make a chance pass more likely; the four offsets and the locked look
are the guard.
