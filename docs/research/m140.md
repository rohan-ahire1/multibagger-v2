# M140: M106 at live capital sizes, through the paper engine (information; written 2026-10-23, before any M140 run)

Twenty-third cycle of the autonomous loop. LOOP step 10 work (realism, M7 prep); no candidate, nothing adopted, the
variant count is unchanged.

## Question

Research runs hold fractional shares and charge the ₹16 depository fee only on a closing sale. The paper book (and a
live book) trades whole shares on raw prices, with orders sized off the decision close. M137 put a floor of about
₹2.5 lakh on live capital (names rounding to 0 shares); M135 saw a +1.34% equity gap at ₹2.5 lakh over ten months.
How much CAGR / drawdown / Calmar does M106 lose over 2010-23 at ₹2.5 lakh, ₹5 lakh, ₹10 lakh, ₹25 lakh and ₹1 crore?
And what does charging the DP fee on every sale day (partial resize sells too; CDSL charges per scrip per day of sale)
add on top?

## Method

`notebooks/m140_live_capital.py` drives `mb.live.paper.advance` (the running books' code) over 2010-01-04..2023-12-31
on `nse_research@2026-10-14` with M106's strategy config (`m120_ref`), one book per capital. Holidays for the paper
calendar are the weekdays with no session in the panel (the NSE list is not on this box). Each book's equity gives
CAGR, max drawdown and Calmar with `equity_metrics`; reference: the fractional backtest, run 20261022-194129-d756637e
(30.09% / 20.06% / 1.500). The DP-fee-per-sale-day view subtracts ₹16 per partial sell fill from equity (not
compounded; a lower bound on its cost).

If a book is blocked (an unexplained jump on a held or picked name), the session and reason are reported and that
capital's result is left out.

## Use

Information for the user's M7 capital decision. No rule change; the running paper books are untouched.

## Results (2026-10-23)

`notebooks/m140_live_capital.py` at code d49c22d (plus the notebook's fund-list fix below); `reports/m140/<capital>.json`.
No book was blocked. Reference: fractional backtest 30.09% / 20.06% / 1.500 (run 20261022-194129-d756637e).

| Start capital | CAGR | Max DD | Calmar | Partial sells | Picks never bought (0 shares) | Calmar, DP fee on every sale day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹2.5 lakh | 29.11% | 20.16% | 1.444 | 1,249 | 19 | 1.438 |
| ₹5 lakh | 29.48% | 20.17% | 1.462 | 1,337 | 0 | 1.459 |
| ₹10 lakh | 29.66% | 20.16% | 1.471 | 1,379 | 0 | 1.470 |
| ₹25 lakh | 29.75% | 20.16% | 1.476 | 1,412 | 0 | 1.475 |
| ₹1 crore | 29.80% | 20.16% | 1.478 | 1,431 | 0 | 1.478 |

- The paper engine reproduces the research book over 14 years: even at ₹1 crore it gives up 0.29 pts of CAGR (orders
  sized off the decision close, the whole-share no-trade band). That is the implementation cost of whole shares, as
  measured on the running books' own code.
- Small capital costs more, but less than feared: ₹2.5 lakh gives up 1.0 pt (19 picks rounded to zero shares, all in
  the first years before the book grew); ₹5 lakh 0.6 pt. Drawdown does not change.
- DP fee on every sale day (₹16 per partial resize sell too, as CDSL charges): at most 0.03 pts of CAGR. The
  backtest's fee rule is good enough.
- Caveat: each book compounds from its start, so a small book grows out of its rounding within a few years; a live
  book that withdraws its gains would keep the small-book drag.
- For M7: ₹5 lakh or more keeps the live book within ~0.6 pts of research; below ₹2.5 lakh names start to drop.

Notebook fix (no engine change): bhavcopies before mid-2011 carry no ISIN, so the paper code alone would rank
GOLDBEES as a stock in 2010 and stop ("fixed_weights symbols were also picked"). The notebook passes the dataset's
fund list into the decision, as research runs do (M35). Live data has ISINs; the running books are not affected.
