# M87: can the stop's sells actually fill? (realism check, information)

The 10% stop sells names that are falling fast, so more of its sells may land on days a stock is locked at its lower
price band (no buyers). M39 counted this for the pin. `notebooks/m87_stop_circuits.py` repeats the count for the stop
books and prices it: a locked-down sell is assumed to fill at the open of the next session that is not locked down,
and the extra loss is charged against the run's equity on that day.

| Run | Exits | Locked down | Extra loss, total / a year | Worst single |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pin `m49_b_b2` (20261002-000607-0103cc7f) | 1,250 | 3 | 1.08% / 0.08 pts | 0.37% |
| stop10 `m72_stop10_b2` (20261002-012910-34c96cab) | 1,670 | 6 | 3.58% / 0.26 pts | 1.03% |
| stop10 off5 / off10 / off15 (`m73_*`) | ~1,680 | 5 / 3 / 6 | 0.16 / 0.12 / 0.17 pts a year | ≤ 1.16% |
| rot_s10 `m78_rot_s10_b2` (20261002-021129-8a695cf6) | 1,707 | 5 | 3.00% / 0.22 pts | 1.36% |
| g0s10 `m86_g0s10_b2` (20261002-030829-77e725bf) | 1,670 | 6 | 4.77% / 0.34 pts | 1.36% |

The same few names drive it (WINPRO Oct 2020, −61% before it could be sold; PCJEWELLER 2023; VAKRANGEE 2018). None
falls in the March 2020 crash, where the stop earns its keep.

**Result:** the stop books lose about 0.1–0.35 points of CAGR a year to unfillable sells, vs 0.08 for the pin. This
trims the stop's edge slightly and changes no conclusion. For paper/live: a stop sell that does not fill must stay
pending and retry each session.
