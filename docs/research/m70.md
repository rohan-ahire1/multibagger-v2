# M70: the half-rotation rule on the out-of-sample walk-forward (information)

Pre-registered 2026-10-02, before any M70 run.

## Why

The pin was tuned on 2010–17 (M42 step B), so its folds 1 and 2 are in-sample, and every rival is compared with an
optimiser's best on those years. M52 removed that bias for the plain book: each period uses only the setting chosen
from earlier data (A for 2014–17, B for 2018–20, C for 2021–23), giving Calmar 0.953 for 2014–23. This applies the
half-rotation rule to the same stitched process. The rule was fixed before M42 (M27 rotation from the papers; 50/50
from M36), so stitching it adds no new choice.

## Method (`notebooks/m70_stitched_half.py`)

For each piece, start from 50% in the selected setting's book (M49 runs `m49_{a,b,c}*_b2`) and 50% in the same setting
with fixed gold replaced by the rotation (`m70_rot_{a,c}*_b2`, new; B's is `m59_rot*_b2`), never rebalanced within the
piece. Chain the pieces' daily returns. Also in four decision-day tranches (offsets 0/5/10/15, equal weights at each
piece start), with the plain book's tranche form beside it. Offset-0 rows compare with M52.

New sleeves: `m70_rot_a{,_off5,_off10,_off15}_b2` and `m70_rot_c{...}_b2` = `m49_a*_b2` / `m49_c*_b2` with
`fixed_weights: {}` and `rotation: {assets: [GOLDBEES], lookbacks_sessions: [21, 63, 126, 252], top_k: 1, cash_floor: false}`.
Nothing is adopted.

## Results (2026-10-02, information)

Sleeve runs in `reports/m70_runs.txt`; the offset-0 plain row reproduces M52 exactly.

| 2014–2023, settings chosen from earlier data only | CAGR | Max DD | Calmar | 2014–17 (A) | 2018–20 (B) | 2021–23 (C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| book, offset 0 (M52) | 24.84% | 26.07% | 0.953 | 1.557 | 0.816 | 2.271 |
| **half rotation, offset 0** | **28.37%** | **23.60%** | **1.202** | 1.786 | 0.796 | 2.003 |
| book, 4 tranches | 25.47% | 26.65% | 0.956 | 1.522 | 0.783 | 2.513 |
| **half rotation, 4 tranches** | **29.16%** | **25.16%** | **1.159** | 1.634 | 0.668 | 2.343 |

- On the honest walk-forward, the half-rotation rule adds about 3.5–3.7 points of CAGR and about 0.2–0.25 Calmar,
  in both the one-day and the four-day form. That is the largest out-of-sample gain in this search.
- Where it comes from: it wins 2014–17 (the bull fold where the in-sample pin is hard to beat) and 2021–23 on return,
  and gives back a little in 2018–20 (2020: the rotation was in the book through the crash, then partly in gold for
  the rebound).
- Caveat: the rotation rule (M27) was first measured on 2010–23 with an older book, so it is not perfectly out of
  sample; its settings come from the papers (Keller's 1/3/6/12 months, 50/50), not from a search.
- Together with 2007–09 (half in tranches 0.617 vs 0.284, M68), this is the strongest evidence yet for the half
  blend over the plain pin. It does not change the formal verdict (M68/M69); it informs the user's choice.
