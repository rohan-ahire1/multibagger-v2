# M142: the M113 pullback blend through the full loop bar (pre-registered 2026-10-25, before any M142 run)

Twenty-fifth cycle of the autonomous loop. Information first, candidate second.

## Why

The largest effect found in all of this research is outside the research branch. On 2026-10-12 a separate line of
work (worktree `~/projects/mb-m108`, branch `m108`, its own milestone numbers M108-M113, not the research branch's
M108-M119) built a short-term pullback book and blended it with M106:

- "A" = M106 0.8 + pullback `m109_pb_n5_mkt_rsi_h7` 0.2, rebalanced to those weights at each month's last close.
- Dev 2010-23 on `nse_research@2026-10-12m108`: Calmar 1.876 (f1 2.478 / f2 1.987 / f3 2.179) vs M106 1.500
  (2.259 / 2.200 / 1.683). It **failed f2** (1.987 vs 2.200).
- Its one `--final` (M113, locked window 2024-01..2026-09): 1.533 (20.37% CAGR / 13.29% DD) vs M106 1.314
  (20.39% / 15.52%). M113 called it "worth paper trading", the user's call.

It was never tested against this loop's bar, which came later: four decision offsets, after-tax Calmar, 25 bps, neighbours, bootstrap.
The pullback sleeve holds names for at most 7 days, so all its gains are short-term (20% STCG plus 4% cess, surcharge ignored, in
`configs/tax/india_fy2026.yaml`); M106 holds names for months, so more of its gains are long-term (12.5%). After tax is where this blend is most likely to lose.
The user needs this before any decision to paper-trade it.

Code: the m108 line's pullback strategy, engine open/limit entries, reclaim and time exits, the monthly-rebalanced
blend and the calendar strategy, brought in by cherry-pick (docs left out because their numbers clash). Tests come with
it (`tests/test_m108_pullback.py`, `test_m110_rebalance_volume.py`, `test_m111_calendar.py`). Suite: 376 passed,
9 skipped.

After tax (`notebooks/m142_tax.py`): each sleeve's book is taxed on its own (FIFO, M110 model; pullback trades
turned into buy / sell fills). The two after-tax curves are then mixed with the blend's monthly rule and costs. Gains
realised by moving money between the sleeves at month-ends are not taxed, which flatters the blend slightly; the
notebook reports the rebalancing turnover so the gap can be judged. The pre-tax mix must reproduce the blend run's curve.

## The bar (LOOP.md, unchanged)

Against M106: (1) Calmar higher before and after tax at offset 0, and before tax at offsets 5/10/15; (2) no fold lost;
(3) higher at 25 bps; (4) neighbours w10 / w30 hold, block bootstrap ≥ 85%.

Expected: (2) fails again at f2 (it did on the older dataset); if so, A cannot be adopted by this bar whatever the
rest shows, and nothing is changed to rescue f2 (that would be fitting the fold). All points are still run and
reported, because the user's paper-trading decision (M113) rests on them. This milestone adds 7 configs to the
variant count (about 1,027), but A itself was counted in the m108 line's 53.

Month-end moves between the sleeves are 0.07x the book a year, so leaving their gains untaxed flatters A very

The bar, point by point:
1. Calmar before and after tax at offset 0, before tax at offsets 5/10/15: **pass** (1.876 / 1.412 vs 1.500 / 1.102;
   1.244 / 1.445 / 1.660 vs 1.080 / 1.273 / 1.409).
2. No fold lost: **fail**. f2 (2014-17) is lost at all four offsets, at 25 bps, and after tax. f1 and f3 are won
   everywhere.
3. 25 bps: **pass** (1.679 vs 1.411), although the sleeve alone falls from 0.741 to 0.428 (CAGR 24.7% → 17.2%).
4. Neighbours hold (w10 1.726, w30 1.832) but the bootstrap is **74.8%**, below 85%: **fail**.

Why f2: in 2014-17 M106 made 30% a year with a 13.7% drawdown. A gives up CAGR there (29.19% vs 30.06%) and its
drawdown is no smaller (14.69% vs 13.66%). A likely reason, not tested: a dip-buying sleeve earns little in a steady
uptrend. The blend's gain comes from shallower drawdowns in f3 and f1. M106's worst drawdown is 20.1% on
2022-05-25; A's worst is 15.6% on 2020-03-23, and A's 2022 drawdown is smaller than M106's. That is a real trade-off, not luck at one offset, which is why the bar (no fold lost) rejects it.

Verdict: not adopted; M106 stays. This is the strongest near miss in the research to date: it wins both whole-period
tests (dev at four offsets, the locked 2024-26 window in M113), before and after tax, at 25 bps. It loses one fold
consistently, and the bootstrap gives it 75%. Paper-trading it beside M106 is the user's call (M113); it would need
the paper engine to run a daily pullback sleeve (limit orders 3% below the close, next-day fills, 7-day exits).
That is not built. Nothing here was tuned towards f2. Variants: about 1,027.

Note on the first tax run: the converter assumed the momentum engine's trade columns (`bought` / `sold`). The
pullback engine's trades have `entry_price` / `pnl`, so the buy is shares x entry price and the sell adds the pnl.
That is fixed in the notebook; no numbers were produced before the fix.
