# M121: a long-term reversal guard on M106's buys (pre-registered 2026-10-23, before any M121 backtest)

Second cycle of the autonomous loop. Research notes: `docs/research/loop-journal.md` (2026-10-22 entries).

## Idea and sources

Past winners over three to five years tend to reverse (De Bondt & Thaler 1985); momentum portfolios give back their
gains in years two to five after formation (Jegadeesh & Titman 2001), and the stocks that carry momentum profits are
the ones that do not later reverse (Conrad & Yavuz 2017, *Review of Finance*). On NSE stocks, Dhankar & Maheshwari
(1997–2013) find short-term momentum together with long-term overreaction. M106 ranks on months 1–12 (273 sessions,
skip 14) and ignores what came before, so a name can be a 12-month winner after already being a top 13–36-month
winner, the "late stage" of the momentum life cycle (Lee & Swaminathan 2000). The guard skips those.

Not tried before: M45 changed the ranking window (12 to 7), M67 blended lookbacks; none used returns before month 12.

## Rule

Long-term return at decision d: adj_close 252 sessions before d / adj_close 756 sessions before d − 1 (months 13–36).
Among the decision's ranked (eligible) names that have it, the top `drop_frac` (ties by symbol) are long-term winners.
Rule (`nosell`, M111's path): a name not already held is not bought if it is a long-term winner; held names and
weights are untouched; the next name takes the slot. Names with under 756 sessions of history are not affected.

Code: `LongReversal`/`ParamsM121` (`params.py`), `long_winners` (`targets.py`), `tests/test_m121_long_reversal.py`.

## Candidates (M106 + one change; dev 2010-01-04..2023-12-31, `nse_research@2026-10-14`)

- `m121_lr10`: drop_frac 0.10.
- `m121_lr20`: drop_frac 0.20.
Each at offsets 0/5/10/15 and 25 bps. Reference: the M120 reference runs (`m120_ref*`, M106 on the same dataset).

Information before any backtest (`notebooks/m121_reversal.py`, no returns after the buy): 9.9% of M106's 1,278 new
buys are in the top 10% (126) and 17.8% in the top 20% (227), about the base rate, spread over all years (5–14 a year
for lr10). So 12-month and 13–36-month winners are nearly unrelated here, and the rule binds in every fold.

## The bar (LOOP.md, unchanged)

Against `m120_ref*`: (1) whole-period Calmar higher before and after tax at offset 0, and before tax at offsets
5/10/15; (2) no fold lost (f1/f2/f3, offset 0, before tax); (3) higher than `m120_ref_slip25` at 25 bps;
(4) robustness: neighbours (drop_frac 0.05/0.15/0.30; window 504 / 1,008 sessions) do not collapse, block bootstrap
Calmar above the reference in ≥ 85% of resamples. If both pass, the higher four-offset mean wins. No `--final` run.

Fail: any point above. Expected: weak. In a small-cap bull market the long-term winners may keep winning
(multibaggers), and the long-term reversal literature is mostly about 3–5-year holding horizons, longer than M106's
typical hold. Variants tried before M121: about 992; M121 adds 2.

What it does: skipping the top long-term winners barely changes CAGR (lr10 30.04% vs 30.09%) and trims the 2018–23
drawdown (19.6% vs 20.1%), but costs 1.3 points of CAGR in 2010–13. The effect is too small to tell from noise; the
long-term reversal guard is not adopted. Not worth neighbour checks: the bootstrap already says the edge is a coin flip.
