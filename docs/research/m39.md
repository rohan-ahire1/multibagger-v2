# M39: fills on price-band (circuit) days

Realism check, not a strategy change (2026-10-01). NSE caps a stock's daily move at a price band (5/10/20%). On a
day locked at the band (high = low), a buy (locked up) or a sell (locked down) cannot fill, but the backtest fills
at the open. The ₹2 crore floor admits smaller names, which hit bands more often. `notebooks/m39_circuits.py`
counts entry and exit fills of the momentum names (GOLDBEES excluded) on such days, 2010–2023.

- Under 1% of fills, and about the same for both floors. A locked-down sell that really fills a few days later at,
  say, another 5–10% lower costs about 11 × 7.5% × 2.5% (one 40-name position) ≈ 2% of equity over 14 years,
  about 0.15 points of CAGR a year. It does not change any conclusion.
- Missing a locked-up buy mostly just delays it.
- Live/paper: the daily job should treat a sell that does not fill (locked down) as pending and retry, and not
  chase a locked-up buy. Worth checking in M7 (Dhan) order handling.

Fills with no EQ bar that day (pin): 85, of which 75 are sells on BE-series days (the stock moved to trade-for-trade;
still sellable, at a price the backtest takes from the BE row) and 10 are names that stopped trading (delisted or

last close, so this is mildly conservative. No action.
