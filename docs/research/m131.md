# M131: equal-risk-contribution weights among the picks (pre-registered 2026-10-23, before any M131 backtest)

Thirteenth cycle of the autonomous loop (M130 was information only).

## Idea and sources

M106 weights its 25 picks by 1 / volatility, which ignores correlation: when several picks move together (one
sector's run, e.g. PSU banks or metals), the book holds that one bet several times over. Equal risk contribution
(ERC, "risk parity"; Maillard, Roncalli & Teiletche 2010, *Journal of Portfolio Management*) weights so that each name
adds the same share of portfolio variance; with equal correlations it is exactly inverse volatility, so the change is
purely the correlation part. Out of sample ERC and related correlation-aware schemes beat inverse-variance or equal
weight on risk (Maillard et al. 2010; López de Prado 2016 *JPM* on HRP; Clarke, de Silva & Thorley 2013 *JPM*). For
momentum books, crowding into one cluster is a known crash channel (Daniel & Moskowitz 2016). Not tried here (grep:
no covariance, risk parity or HRP rule); the sector cap (M72) is the blunt, failed version of the same idea.

## Rule

At each decision, for the picked stocks (gold sleeve and risk-off switch unchanged): daily log returns over the last
84 market sessions (M106's `inverse_vol_sessions`; a day a name did not trade counts as 0, fewer than 42 traded days
is an error), sample covariance; each off-diagonal correlation is shrunk toward the picks' mean correlation by
`erc_shrink`; ERC weights by cyclical coordinate descent (Griveau-Billion, Richard & Roncalli 2013). These replace
the inverse-vol weights. Code: `PortfolioM131`, `erc`, `erc_weights` (`targets.py`); `tests/test_m131_erc.py`.
Note: M106's inverse-vol uses each name's own last 84 rows; ERC uses the last 84 market sessions (same for names that
traded every day).
