# M120: frequent upper-circuit stocks as a buy filter on M106 (pre-registered 2026-10-22, before any M120 backtest)

First cycle of the autonomous loop (`docs/research/LOOP.md`). Research notes: `docs/research/loop-journal.md`.

## Idea and sources

In markets with daily price limits the usual lottery measure (largest daily return, MAX) understates the lottery
features of a stock, because limits cap the extreme days; measures built from how often a stock hits its limit predict
lower future returns (Yao, Wang, Cui & Fang 2021, *International Review of Economics & Finance* 73, China; Wang & Lien
2022, *Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting* 59, Taiwan, a modified MAX from up- and down-limit hit rates; Lin,
Qiu & Zheng 2023, *Journal of Banking & Finance* 150, price-limit exposure draws attention and lowers returns). Indian
practitioners running momentum books skip stocks that hit circuits often ("more than 20 times") as speculative and hard
to trade. NSE stocks outside F&O have 2/5/10/20% bands; a stock living at its upper band is an operator or retail
frenzy name, and those are what M106's small-cap picks are exposed to.

Why this is not the dead MAX family: M72 tested a MAX filter (largest daily return over 21 sessions) on an older pin.
The papers above say exactly that MAX misses the limit-capped days; this rule counts them over a year. M40/M106's
delivery filter is related (speculative volume) but is a share of volume, not a price-limit count.

## Rule

An up-limit hit is a session where the raw close equals the raw high and the close-to-close move (adj_close) is within
[band − 0.25%, band + 0.10%] of 5%, 10% or 20%. The 2% band is left out: at 2% a close at the high is common without
any limit. The window was set from the distribution of close-at-high moves on the whole panel (no returns used): clear
peaks at 4.8–5.0%, 9.8–10.0%, 19.9%, background about a fifth of the peak bins.
Signal at a decision d: the count of hits in the last 252 sessions to d (fewer sessions if the symbol is newer).
Rule (`nosell`, M111's code path): a name not already held is not bought if its count is above `max_hits`; held names
and weights are untouched; the next eligible name takes the slot.

Code: `LimitHits`/`ParamsM120` in `src/mb/strategy/momentum/params.py`, `limit_hits` in `targets.py`, tests in
`tests/test_m120_limit_hits.py`, numbers from `notebooks/m120_limits.py`.

## Candidates (M106 + one change each; dev 2010-01-04..2023-12-31, dataset `nse_research@2026-10-14`)

- `m120_lh20`: max_hits 20 (the practitioner number).
- `m120_lh10`: max_hits 10.
Each at offsets 0/5/10/15 (`_offN`) and 25 bps (`_slip25`). Reference: `m120_ref*` = M106 on the same dataset (the
M119 amendment checked that `2026-10-14` equals `2026-10-02b` for M106's prices; the reference re-runs confirm it here,
since `runs/` from the Mac is not on this machine).

Information, before any backtest (`notebooks/m120_limits.py info`, no returns): of M106's 1,278 new buys in 2010–23,
the count is 0 for the median buy; 18.6% have more than 10, 13.4% more than 20. The rule binds in 2010, 2014, 2020–22
(2021: 72 of 109 buys above 10) and almost never in 2011–13 or 2019. So it mostly acts on the 2020–22 small-cap boom,
fold f3; f1 and f2 will barely move.

## The bar (LOOP.md, unchanged)

A candidate passes only if, against `m120_ref*`:
1. whole-period Calmar higher before and after tax (M110 model) at offset 0, and before tax at offsets 5, 10, 15;
2. no development fold lost (f1, f2, f3 before tax at offset 0);
3. higher Calmar than `m120_ref_slip25` at 25 bps;
4. robustness: neighbours (max_hits ±5, sessions 126 / 504) do not collapse; stationary block bootstrap (M20 settings:
   block 63, 5,000 resamples) Calmar above the reference in ≥ 85% of resamples.
If both pass, the one with the higher mean before-tax Calmar over the four offsets is the result. No `--final` run.

Fail: any point above. Expected: a small effect at best; the risk is that 2021's best momentum names were exactly the
circuit names, so f3 may fall. Variants tried before M120: about 990 (M104's ~900 plus ~90 in M110–M119); M120 adds 2.
