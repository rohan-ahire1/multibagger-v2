# M76: trailing and volatility-widened stops

## Question

M73's 10% stop (from the rebalance open, Han, Zhou & Zhu 2016) cut the worst drawdowns but lost to the pin in
2010–13 and 2014–17, where single names dipped 10% and then recovered (whipsaw). Two standard changes:

- **Trailing stop** (Kaminski & Lo 2014; Lo & Remorov 2017): the reference ratchets up to the highest close since the
  rebalance, so gains made during the month are protected and a fall from a peak counts.
- **Volatility-widened stop** (ATR-style stops used by trend followers): a name's stop is max(10%, 4.5 × its daily
  return std over 63 sessions). 4.5 was set from the median 63-session std of the pin's holdings at entry (2.2%, so
  4.5σ ≈ 10%; measured on 2010–23 entries, results not looked at). Volatile names get room; calm ones keep 10%.

New engine options in ParamsM76 (`exits.stop_trailing`, `exits.stop_vol_sessions`, `exits.stop_vol_mult`).

| Candidate | Change from the pin |
|---|---|
| `m76_trail10_b2` | 10% trailing stop |
| `m76_trail15_b2` | 15% trailing stop (a trailing stop fires more often than a fixed one at the same level) |
| `m76_vol45_b2` | stop max(10%, 4.5 × std over 63 sessions), from the rebalance open |

Three variants. Known before: M72/M73 fixed stops (10% best of 10/15/20; tranches 1.513, folds 1.492 / 2.142 / 1.773).

Same checks and bar as M74/M75: the pin (1.125; 1.598 / 2.072 / 1.362) and baselines; then offsets 3 of 4 above the
pin's 1.101 / 1.055 / 1.142; 25 bps above 1.078 (1.498 / 1.971 / 1.310); 2007–09 mean ≥ 0.284; tranches above the pin's
tranches (1.105; 1.590 / 2.199 / 1.312). Compared, as information, with M73's fixed 10% stop.
