# M89: the 200-day filter's risk-off share in gold instead of cash

## Question

M88 (seen before this pre-registration): the no-gold stop book with the 200-day NIFTYBEES filter makes the best
whole-period Calmar so far (risk-off half: 31.2% CAGR, 16.2% DD, 1.928; tranches 1.698), but loses fold 1 (2010–13:
17.5% CAGR vs the pin's 20.9%). The pin wins 2010–13 with gold: in 2011 Indian stocks fell ~25% while gold rose ~30%
in rupees. M89 puts the money the filter takes out of stocks into GOLDBEES instead of cash, so gold is held only when
the market trend is down (unlike the pin's fixed 25%).

## New option (`ParamsM89`)

`regime.risk_off_symbol`: null keeps today's behaviour (risk-off share in cash). A symbol: on a decision date when the
benchmark is below its average, the share left uninvested (1 − gross) buys that symbol; the picks keep `gross`. Needs
`regime.enabled`. Not combinable with `exits.book_stop_frac`. Paper trading refuses it.

| Candidate | Change from M88 |
|---|---|
| `m89_g0s10_r0g_b2` | `m88_g0s10_r0_b2` + `risk_off_symbol: GOLDBEES` (all of the book to gold when risk-off) |
| `m89_g0s10_r50g_b2` | `m88_g0s10_r50_b2` + `risk_off_symbol: GOLDBEES` (half to gold) |

Gold is under the same 10% stop as the stocks (as in every stop book since M72). Two variants. Same checks and bar as
M73–M88, including tranches vs the pin's tranches. Expected: f1 closer to the pin; some cost in 2022 and early 2020,
when gold fell with stocks.
