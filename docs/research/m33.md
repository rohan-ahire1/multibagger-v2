# M33: a lower liquidity floor

Pre-registration (2026-10-01, Claude), written and committed before any run. Development period
2010-01-04..2023-12-31 only; no `--final`. Dataset `nse_research@2026-10-01`.

## Why

Every momentum config so far uses a ₹5 crore 50-session average daily turnover floor (`min_adv_inr: 50000000`), set
in M5 for a fund-sized book and never varied. The live book is ₹10 lakh: with 40 names, a position is about ₹25,000.
Momentum is stronger in less-traded stocks: Lee & Swaminathan (Journal of Finance 2000) find low-volume winners keep
winning longer than high-volume ones; Indian studies of momentum and liquidity (e.g. Sehgal & Jain; a 2006–2025 NSE
backtest of the Nifty 200 Momentum 30 split by turnover/market cap, freefincal 2025) find the gain concentrated in the
less liquid half. Funds cannot hold these names at size; a small book can. A floor that lets more of them in should
raise CAGR; the question is whether Calmar rises too.

## Runs (question `momentum-m33-liquidity`, against the pin `momentum/m19_gold20_invvol_b2`, Calmar 0.712)

`m31_gold20_invvol_b2` (the pin on this dataset, same numbers) with only `min_adv_inr` changed:

| Config | min_adv_inr |
|---|---|
| `m33_adv2cr_gold20_b2` | 20,000,000 (₹2 crore) |
| `m33_adv1cr_gold20_b2` | 10,000,000 (₹1 crore) |

₹1 crore keeps every position under about 0.25% of a day's turnover at today's book; it is the lowest floor that
still leaves the book room to grow. The ₹50 price floor and EQ-series filter stay. No other variants.

Adoption as always: beat the pin and both baselines on Calmar, whole period and every fold. A passing config then
faces, in order, and is recommended only if it passes all of them:

1. Offsets 5/10/15: win at least 3 of 4 against the gold20 offset runs.
2. Costs: 25 bps slippage a side (`nse_cash_slip25`) against `m20_gold20_slip25_b2`. Thinner names have wider
   spreads, so this check matters more here.
3. Capacity (the M13/M20 measure in a notebook): p95 fill as a share of the day's turnover at a ₹10 lakh book, and the
   book size at which it reaches 5%. It must be at least ₹1 crore.
4. Bootstrap: beat the pin in at least 80% of resamples.

## Disclosure

- Known before: all results to M32. No run with a different `min_adv_inr` has been made or looked at.
- Risks: thinner names have more data errors; the unexplained-jump gate may refuse a run, which would be reported
  and fixed as data (as in M31), not by changing the floor. In a crash thin names fall harder, so the drawdown may grow.

### Verdict

Adopted: **`m33_adv2cr_gold20_b2`** (₹2 crore floor) becomes the research reference, pinned as
`momentum/m33_adv2cr_gold20_b2`. It gives up a little Calmar against ₹1 crore at 10 bps (0.865 vs 0.881) but holds up
better under higher costs, has 1.7× the capacity and wins every check. The ₹1 crore floor is the higher-return
option for a book under about ₹1 crore that can trade with limit orders.

Live note: a ₹2 crore floor reaches names whose spreads are wider than the Nifty 500's; paper/live should use limit
orders near the open. Capacity of about ₹2 crore is far above today's ₹10 lakh book.
