# M104: deflated Sharpe ratio of the lead books (information)

## Question

M95 was found after ~900 runs on the same 2010–23 data. How much of its edge could be the best of many tries?
Bailey & López de Prado (2014): the deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) is the probability that the true Sharpe ratio beats the
best one would expect from N unskilled tries, allowing for skew and fat tails. `notebooks/m104_deflated_sharpe.py`.
Trials: every momentum or blend run on 2010-01-04..2023-12-31, one per config hash (893 after dropping the pin's
own tranches as base). The tries are highly correlated, so N is also shown as an effective count
N / (1 + (N − 1)ρ). No new settings, no bar; information only.

## Results

Level (daily returns, annualised Sharpe; 894 trials, mean correlation 0.80, effective N 1.3; best-of-N by luck 1.36):

| Book | Run | Sharpe | DSR (N) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M95 tranches | 20261002-041639-baa42e53 | 2.49 | 1.000 |
| M102 7.5% stop tranches | 20261002-045215-4e3e6d98 | 2.52 | 1.000 |
| M98 top 20 tranches | 20261002-043350-fef356b4 | 2.46 | 1.000 |
| Pin | 20261002-000607-0103cc7f | 2.24 | 0.998 |

Every book here clearly makes money (all momentum books did in 2010–23). The useful question is the gain **over the
pin**. Same test on the daily return difference against the pin's tranches (run 20261002-001643-d6a3c453); 893
trials, mean correlation 0.21, effective N 4.7; best-of-N by luck: 1.64 (N), 0.59 (effective N):

| Book minus pin tranches | Information ratio | PSR (> 0) | DSR (N) | DSR (effective N) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M95 tranches | 0.56 | 0.984 | 0.000 | 0.456 |
| M102 7.5% stop tranches | 0.24 | 0.818 | 0.000 | 0.093 |
| M98 top 20 tranches | 0.65 | 0.994 | 0.000 | 0.592 |

**Reading:** on its own, M95's extra return over the pin would be 98% likely real; after allowing for the tries it
is about a coin flip (0.46 with the effective count, near zero with the raw count). M98 (top 20) is slightly firmer
(0.59). The test measures mean return only. M95's main gain is lower drawdown (20% vs 27%) from the filter, which
this test does not score and which rests on a handful of episodes (2011, 2015–16, 2018, 2020, 2022), so it cannot
be made significant on 14 years either. Practical meaning: treat the 33% CAGR / 1.66 Calmar as an upper estimate;
the stitched walk-forward (M96: 1.661) and M52's realistic range (Calmar ~1.0–1.1 for the plain book) bracket what to
expect, and only the locked 2024+ period can settle it.
