# M30: rank by high-to-price momentum (the crash-free part of momentum)

Pre-registration (2026-10-01, Claude), written and committed before the code is used for any run. Development period
2010-01-04..2023-12-31 only; no `--final`.

## Why

Büsing, Mohrschladt & Siedhoff, "Decomposing Momentum: Eliminating its Crash Component" (Journal of Banking & Finance
168, 2024; SSRN 3401656; SFA 2023 best paper in investments) split the 12-1 momentum return at the highest price inside
the formation window: HTP = log(high / price at the start), the run-up to the peak, and PTH = log(price at the end /
high), the move since the peak. Momentum = HTP + PTH. They report that HTP earns about 84% of momentum's profit and
that the crashes come from PTH; a strategy on HTP alone shows no negative skew. M10's 52-week-high rule (George &
Hwang 2004) ranked on nearness to the high, which is the PTH side, and failed. HTP is the other side.

Caveat taken from the paper's design: their result is for long–short deciles in US data, where crashes come mostly
from the short leg. This book is long-only, so the gain, if any, has to come from the winners it holds.

## The rule (`RankingM29.order: htp`)

`order: htp` scores each name by HTP = log(H / P0), with P0 = adj_close `lookback_sessions` before the decision date
and H = the highest adj_close over the rows from P0 to `skip_sessions` before the decision date (both ends included).
With `score: return_over_vol` it is divided by the same volatility the pin uses (stdev of daily log returns over the
lookback-to-skip span). Same restrictions as `low_vol` (no other ranking option).
