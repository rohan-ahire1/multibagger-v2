# M139: short NIFTY futures instead of the gold switch in risk-off (pre-registered 2026-10-23, before any M139 backtest)

Twenty-second cycle of the autonomous loop.

## Idea and sources

When JUNIORBEES closes below its 200-day average at a decision, M106 sells half its stock share (37.5% of the book) and
buys GOLDBEES. That cuts market exposure, but it also gives up the stock picks' return over the market (the momentum
selection return) on that half. A beta hedge keeps every pick and shorts index futures instead (portable alpha:
selection return plus the futures' carry in place of the market).

Evidence: Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, JFE, "Momentum crashes"): after market falls, past winners are low-beta stocks
and the crash risk sits in the short (loser) side, which a long-only book does not hold; their up-market beta asymmetry
in bear markets comes from the losers. So shorting the index against long winners in bear states is a hedge of the
market part, not of a crash in winners. No paper tests a futures overlay on a long-only momentum book; this is a
mechanism test, not a replication. Gold was the best risk-off asset in India in 2010-23 (M89 vs M88: gold beat cash
in the switch), so the expected result is unclear: the hedge swaps 37.5% gold for (picks − NIFTY + carry).

Not a retry of a dead family: stops, vol targeting and regime-signal variants changed *when* or *how much* to cut;
this keeps M106's signal and size and changes only the instrument.

Practical limits (written now, whatever the result): one NIFTY lot is ~₹18 lakh notional (75 × ~24,000), so a 37.5%
hedge needs a book of ≳ ₹50 lakh; smaller books would round to 0 or 1 lot. Daily mark-to-market losses on the short
are paid in cash, and SEBI's 50% cash-collateral rule means part of the margin must be cash or liquid funds; the test
ignores both (the book's idle cash earns 0 in `nse_cash`, so the error is small). Futures P&L is business income
(slab rate) for tax; a loss can be set off against capital gains in the same year.

## Data

NSE F&O bhavcopy, NIFTY index futures (`FUTIDX`, `NIFTY`). `data/raw/nse_fo_bhav/` has 2009-2020 (copied from
india-market-data); 2021-2023 are fetched now with a new `mb data fetch fo-bhav` (legacy `fo<DD><MON><YYYY>bhav.csv.zip`
archive, one immutable parquet per year in the same schema). Prices: contract close.

## Rule

Base book: M106 (`m120_ref`) with `regime.enabled: false` (always 75% picks + 25% GOLDBEES). Overlay (new strategy kind
`hedged`, base config pinned by hash):
- Signal: M106's own (JUNIORBEES adj_close < its 200-session SMA at the decision session). Same decision dates as the
  base (its `schedule.decision_offset_sessions`).
- Risk-off at decision d: at the next session's open, short NIFTY futures with notional = ratio × book equity at d's
  close (book = base equity + hedge cash). Risk-on: close any short at the next open.
- Contract: the nearest expiry with more than `roll_sessions` sessions left; roll to the next at the close when it
  reaches that. The rupee notional is kept at each roll.
- P&L: −contracts × change in the futures price, daily; cost `cost_bps_per_side` of the notional on every open, close
  and each leg of a roll (2 bps: STT 0.01-0.0125% on the sell leg, exchange and SEBI fees, a tick of spread).
- Hedge cash sits beside the base book (earns 0, like the base's cash).
Fractional contracts (no lot rounding; see the limits above).

## The bar (LOOP.md, unchanged)

Against `m120_ref*`: (1) Calmar higher before and after tax at offset 0 (after tax: hedge P&L taxed at 30% + cess in
its year, a hedge loss offsetting that year's capital gains first), and before tax at offsets 5/10/15; (2) no fold
lost; (3) higher at 25 bps extra slippage on the stocks and 10 bps a side on the futures; (4) neighbours (ratio 0.25 /
0.5 for h375) hold, and the block bootstrap is ≥ 85%. No `--final`.
Pre-screen first (`notebooks/prescreen.py m120_ref m139_h375 m139_h75`): any offset or fold lost ends the test.

Fail: any point above. Variants tried before M139: about 1,016; M139 adds 2.
