# M138: moving-average distance in the rank (pre-registered 2026-10-23, before any M138 backtest)

Twenty-first cycle of the autonomous loop.

## Idea and sources

Avramov, Kaplanski & Subrahmanyam (2021, Review of Financial Economics 39(2), "Moving average distance as a predictor
of equity returns"; SSRN 3111334): MAD = 21-day SMA / 200-day SMA of a stock's price. Sorting on it earns ~9% a
year in value-weighted alphas (US 1977-2018) beyond momentum, the 52-week high and profitability. The 2023 revision
reports that UMD (12-1 momentum) is insignificant once a MAD factor is in the model, and the authors report it also
predicts in international settings (country tables not seen). Their explanation is anchoring: investors under-react to
the distance from a long average.

Why this is not a plain retry of the dead ranking family (M127 tilt, M128 downside, M129 acceleration): those changed
how the 12-1 window's return is scored. MAD is a different measure that the source says subsumes 12-1 momentum. It
uses the last month too (no skip), which M106's score leaves out. Prior from this project: ranking changes so far
traded CAGR for drawdown or won only at month-end (M127-M129), and the recent month alone flips sign by decision day
(M123). So the expected result is a fail. The test is cheap and uses data we have.

Dropped this cycle at the information stage (not counted): risk-off share in gold only while gold trends up (M137
note: 5 of 45 risk-off month-ends).

## Rule

MAD = mean adj_close over the last 21 sessions / mean over the last 200 sessions, both ending at the decision
session. Among eligible names (after all of M106's filters), the order key is (1 − w) × the percentile rank of
M106's score plus w × the percentile rank of MAD. The top 25 and the hold rule use that order. Everything else is
M106. Code: `RankingM138.mad_rank_weight / mad_short_sessions / mad_long_sessions` in `ranked`; tests in
`tests/test_m138_mad.py`.
