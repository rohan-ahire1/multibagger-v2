# M10 research: three published momentum refinements, tested on both paper books

Pre-registration, written and committed before any tagged run (2026-09-30, Claude working alone overnight at the user's
request). Development period 2010-01-04..2023-12-31 only; the locked period is not touched (no `--final`). Dataset
`nse_research@2026-09-29f` (see the amendment below). Adoption metric: Calmar (`configs/baselines.yaml`).

## Why these three

M5 and M8 tried 11 momentum variants on this development period (regime filter, vol-adjusted ranking, rank buffer,
20/40 names, no resizing, 6-month mix, sector cap, market-vol target). Vol-adjusted ranking and 40 names were kept.
The book's weak spots are its drawdown (about 40%) and its weak 2010–13 fold (Calmar 0.26). The three ideas below are
the best-documented refinements of plain momentum that need only prices, and none is a variant of something already
tried here:

| Idea | Source | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **52-week-high** | George & Hwang (2004), *The 52-Week High and Momentum Investing*, J. Finance | Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past return, and its profits do not reverse |
| **Residual momentum** | Blitz, Huij & Martens (2011), *Residual Momentum*, J. Empirical Finance | Momentum in stock-specific (market-removed) returns has about half the volatility of plain momentum and smaller crashes |
| **Frog in the pan** | Da, Gurun & Warachka (2014), *Frog in the Pan: Continuous Information and Momentum*, Rev. Financial Studies | Among winners, those that rose through many small gains keep going; those that rose through a few jumps do not |

## Variants (six configs, no others)

Each changes only the ranking of its book. Everything else (universe, 12-1 window, equal weights, monthly rebalance,
names held) stays as the book has it.

- **`m10_high52_*`**: score = adjusted close / highest adjusted high over the last **252** sessions (including the
  decision date). Many names sit at exactly 1.0 in strong markets, so ties are broken by the book's usual vol-adjusted
  12-1 score.
- **`m10_residual_*`**: for each stock, fit daily log return = α + β × NIFTYBEES daily log return by least squares over
  the **756** sessions (36 months) ending 21 sessions before the decision date. Residuals e = r − α − β·r_m over the
  12-1 span (the last 231 of those sessions). Score = sum(e) / stdev(e). A stock needs 777 sessions of history.
- **`m10_frog_*`**: take the top **2 × top_n** names by the book's usual score. Order them by information discreteness,
  lowest first: ID = sign(12-1 return) × (share of days with a negative return − share with a positive return), over
  the 12-1 span. Ties by the usual score. The rest follow in the usual order.

`*` is `b1` (book 1, 30 names) or `b2` (book 2, 40 names). The numbers in bold are the papers' own choices, fixed here
and not tuned.

Control: `m10_control_b1` sets every new option off. Its metrics must equal book 1's pin exactly before any variant is
read. It is a check, not a variant.

## Decision rule

Two questions, one per book: `momentum-m10-b1` (against `momentum/voladj_stocks_dev_f`) and `momentum-m10-b2` (against
`momentum/m8_top40_stocks_dev_f`). Per book, the usual bar: beat the pin and both baselines on Calmar on the whole
period and on each of the three folds.

An idea is **recommended** only if all three hold:
1. It passes the bar on both books.
2. Neighbour check: the same idea with its main number halved-ish and doubled-ish also beats book 1's pin on
   whole-period Calmar. Neighbours: 52-week-high window 126 and 504; residual β window 504 and 1008; frog candidates
   1.5 × and 3 × top_n. These four-to-six runs are written only for ideas that pass (1), and tagged
   `momentum-m10-neighbours`.
3. Its gain does not come from one fold alone (reported).

If more than one idea is recommended, the one with the higher book-1 whole-period Calmar is the candidate. No
combinations of ideas are tried in M10. A recommended idea would join paper trading as an extra book only on the user's
say-so; the running books are not changed.

Also reported, not used to decide: CAGR, max drawdown, turnover, average names held, per-fold Calmar.

## Disclosure

- Momentum variants tried on this development period before M10: 11 (M5 5, M8 6). M10 adds 6 (plus neighbours for any
  passer). With about 17 tries on one period, a small win is likely luck. Forward paper trading remains the real test.
- Both books' own development numbers are known (m5.md, m8.md, m8_vcp.md). The three ideas were chosen from the
  literature before any M10 number was computed; no M10 idea has been run on this data before this note was committed.
- The papers use US data, monthly returns and long-short portfolios; here they are long-only on NSE daily data.

- **52-week-high** helps the weak 2010–13 fold (0.40 vs 0.26) but churns twice as much and gives up a third of the
  CAGR in 2018–23: names at their high in a strong market are many, and the tie-break does not stop them rotating.
- **Residual momentum** is close to the book but worse in 2010–13 (0.15). It is not a drawdown fix here: max DD 40.65%.
- **Frog in the pan** matches book 1 on whole-period Calmar (0.644 vs 0.641) with a 3-point smaller drawdown, but it
  gives up 2014–17 (1.37 vs 1.61) and 2010–13; on book 2 it is below the pin. Not a pass, and a 0.003 edge after 17
  tries is noise.
- All six lose in 2014–17, the book's best fold: the plain vol-adjusted 12-1 rank is hard to beat when momentum works.
